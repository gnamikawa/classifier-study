# Context

The project's shared language: how to write about it, and what its words mean.
Tests are grouped by the terms defined here.

## Working style

Answers are short and in plain language. A few sentences beats a structured
write-up; length is earned only when the subject genuinely needs it, never spent
on restating or on options that won't be pursued. The length rule is for
conversation — ADRs, research notes and the definitions below are reference
material and stay as long as they must be.

A sentence must stand on its own. Never let an issue number, a pull request
number, a section marker or a file path do the work of an explanation: say the
thing, then cite. "Issue #46 settled this" tells the reader nothing they can act
on; "the audit found the role names are the vendor's own" does, and the number
can follow it in parentheses. Assume the reader has not read what is being cited
and will not go and read it now.

Define specialist terms on first use. If a reader would need a glossary to parse
the sentence, either reword it or gloss the term in a clause before using it.
When the point turns on specific wording in a file, quote that wording into the
sentence itself; a line number is a pointer, not an explanation. These rules
apply to the repository's prose (ADRs, research notes, commit messages) as well
as to conversation.
